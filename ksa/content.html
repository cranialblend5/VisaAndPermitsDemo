Chapter 01 · The problem

Three systems, one employee, nobody joining them up.

Saudi Arabia is the hardest market in the GCC to govern, and it is not because the rules are unclear. It is because the rules live in three different places, each with its own clock, and one of them can switch the others off.

In plain words Qiwa says whether someone can work. Muqeem says whether they can stay. Absher is what they see on their phone. If those three disagree, your employee finds out at the airport.

The band gates everything

Drop a Nitaqat band and block visas stop, Iqama renewals stop, transfers stop. Most companies find out after it happens, from a rejection in Qiwa, not before it, from their own numbers.

The 90-day clock starts on arrival

An employee lands and you have a statutory window to issue the Iqama. Nobody is counting it for you, and the penalty is not a warning letter.

Exit re-entry is a trap

Someone travels, the visa expires while they are away, and the Iqama is cancelled. They cannot come back. It is the single most expensive administrative mistake in the Kingdom.

The levy is a real number

The expat levy per employee per year is budgeted once and then forgotten. It lands as a payment nobody forecast, in a month nobody planned for.

Renewals pile up together

Iqamas cluster around the dates people were hired. One quarter is quiet, the next has three hundred renewals and the fees to match.

SuccessFactors does not know

Your HR system holds the employee. Qiwa and Muqeem hold the truth about whether they may legally work. Nothing keeps those two in agreement.

Chapter 05 · Exit & re-entry

The process that costs the most when it goes wrong.

Every expatriate who leaves the Kingdom and intends to come back needs an exit re-entry visa. It sounds like admin. It is the single highest-consequence process you run, because the penalty for missing a date is that your employee cannot return at all.

In plain words Your employee goes home to see family. If they are not back before the visa runs out, their Iqama is cancelled and they are stuck outside. We count the days so nobody has to.

What the system does

  • Employee raises the request in self-service with their travel dates
  • Line manager approves it against the leave calendar
  • HR checks eligibility: Iqama validity, outstanding dues, loans, notice
  • PRO applies on Muqeem for single or multiple, and picks the duration
  • SADAD fee captured with the receipt, before the visa is issued
  • Visa number and validity window recorded against the employee
  • Return-by date is calculated from the recorded departure, not the application
  • Alerts fire at 14, 7 and 2 days, to the employee, the line manager and the HRBP
  • Re-entry confirmed on arrival, and the case closes

What goes wrong without it

  • The visa is issued but nobody records when the employee actually left
  • The return-by date is calculated from the wrong day and is quietly wrong
  • An employee extends their trip and nobody tells the PRO
  • The Iqama expires while they are abroad, so the re-entry is invalid anyway
  • A multiple-entry visa is bought when a single would have done, or the reverse
  • Nobody knows how many people are outside the Kingdom right now
  • The first anyone hears of a problem is a call from an airport

Chapter 06 · Saudization & Nitaqat

The number that decides whether you can hire at all.

Nitaqat scores every establishment on the share of Saudi nationals it employs and puts it in a band. The band is not a report card. It is a switch. Fall into the wrong one and Qiwa stops issuing block visas, stops renewing Iqamas and stops accepting transfers.

In plain words The government counts how many Saudis you employ and gives you a colour. Green means you can hire. Red means you cannot. Most companies only find out the colour changed after it changed.

We calculate it before Qiwa does

Headcount, nationality and the weighting rules are already in the system. The band is recalculated continuously, so you see the number moving rather than the result.

We show the headroom

Not just the band, but how far you are from the edge of it. "Thirty-three Saudi leavers would drop us" is a sentence that changes a resignation conversation.

We simulate before you commit

Before a restructure, a redundancy round or a bulk hire, see what it does to the band and to every visa service that depends on it.

Chapter 07 · Tasks & accountability

Put a name on a task and it behaves differently.

Every step becomes a real task with a person, a due date and somewhere to escalate. In the Kingdom that matters more than anywhere, because several of these deadlines are statutory.

In plain words The system never says "someone should do this." It says "Rashid needs to do this by Thursday," and if Thursday passes it tells his manager.
Step 1 · Assign

Somebody owns it

Routed to a role, then claimed by a named officer. Priority and due date are set right then, from the statutory deadline rather than a guess.

Step 2 · Nudge

Reminder every 2 days

Anything still open pings the owner again. Iqama expiries go out six weeks ahead; exit re-entry returns at 14, 7 and 2 days.

Step 3 · Escalate

The manager hears about it

Past the due date it escalates to the line manager and the HRBP on its own. For an overdue return, that happens while the person is still abroad.

Step 4 · Prove

It is all on the record

Who had it, when they were reminded, what they uploaded and when they submitted. A Qiwa or GOSI inspection becomes a report.

Chapter 09 · Healthcare & professional permits

A nurse needs SCFHS before an Iqama is any use.

In Saudi healthcare, the Iqama gets them into the country. The Saudi Commission for Health Specialties decides whether they can treat anyone. That runs on its own clock through Mumaris+, and it is long.

In plain words One badge gets you in. A second badge lets you near a patient. The second one takes months, so it has to start before they arrive, not after.

The SCFHS lane

  • Mumaris+ profile created and the credential pack uploaded
  • DataFlow primary-source verification submitted and tracked to a result
  • Professional classification: GP, specialist, senior registrar, consultant
  • Prometric exam booked, sat and passed, certificate attached
  • Registration completed once they are employed, licence issued
  • Renewal driven by CME hours, with alerts well ahead of expiry

Why it has to start early

Classification through to registration commonly runs several months. Run it after arrival and you are paying someone who cannot work:

  • A clinician on a valid Iqama who cannot be rostered
  • Verification stalled with nobody watching the queue
  • An exam date missed and rebooked a quarter later
  • CME hours logged after the renewal window closed
  • The facility licence expiring underneath everyone working on it

Facility permits too

Hospital, clinic and pharmacy licences, civil defence certificates and municipality permits tracked as establishment documents with their own expiry alerts.

Any industry permit

The same model handles HCIS and industrial safety permits, vehicle registrations, professional memberships and equipment certificates.

Two clocks, one case

The Iqama and the SCFHS licence renew on different cycles. Both sit on the same employee record so neither is missed.

Chapter 10 · Solution architecture

Every system, and what moves between them.

The whole landscape on one picture. SuccessFactors stays your system of record for people. We handle the permit work. Finance gets the money. The Saudi platforms sit on the right, and today that connection is a person, not an API.

In plain words Four corners. Your HR system at the top, the Saudi platforms on the right, finance at the bottom, your people on the left. We are the bit in the middle that keeps them talking.

What flows in

Candidates who accepted an offer, the employee master, separations, and the Iqama expiry dates that start a renewal. Through SAP Integration Suite, so it is monitored and retried like any other SAP integration.

What flows back

Iqama number, expiry, profession as registered in Qiwa, and work permit status, straight into Employee Central. Nobody re-types anything.

What flows down

Every payment with its amount, VAT, SADAD reference, receipt and GL account, posted to S/4HANA as a journal. Levy accruals included.
