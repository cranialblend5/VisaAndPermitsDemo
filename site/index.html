Chapter 01 · The problem

A visa isn’t one job. It’s forty small ones, spread across six people.

One work permit passes through recruitment, onboarding, the PRO desk, the employee, the HR business partner and finance. It crosses a government portal at least five times. Miss a single hand-off and the whole thing just stops. Usually nobody notices until a fine turns up.

In plain words Think of a relay race where the runners can’t see each other and nobody’s holding the list. That’s how most companies run visas today. We hand everyone the same list, and blow a whistle when someone’s late.

The tracking is a spreadsheet

Expiry dates sit in a file on one person’s laptop. When they go on leave, the company is blind. And overstay fines start at the border, not in your system.

The PRO has no digital desk

Your government relations officers run all of it from WhatsApp, email and memory. No queue, no due dates, and no proof of who was asked what, or when.

The money is untraceable

Typing fees, medicals, insurance and ID charges go on company cards and petty cash. Receipts turn up weeks later, if they turn up at all. You find the duplicates at audit.

Recruitment and PRO don't share a clock

Onboarding promises a joining date. Nobody has told the PRO the file exists. Meanwhile the candidate is sitting in another country, waiting on an entry permit that was never started.

Compliance is a memory test

Quota, skill classification, profession matching, work location. Every one of those is a rule somebody has to remember correctly, every single time, usually in a hurry.

Nobody can answer "where is it?"

Your CHRO asks about one employee. Three phone calls and a day later you have an answer. Now multiply that by a few thousand permits across several countries.

Chapter 06 · The money trail

Nothing gets paid without a receipt and a GL code.

Government fees are where the money leaks. Paid on cards, in cash, through Noqodi or e-Dirham, by whoever happens to be at the counter. We make the payment part of the task itself, so money can’t move without leaving a trace.

In plain words You can’t finish the step until you’ve told the system what you paid, how you paid it, and attached the receipt. That way the receipt never goes missing.

Chapter 07 · Country packs

One engine, seven rulebooks.

Labour law is national, so the process has to be too. The engine underneath stays the same everywhere: same tasks, same payment capture, same reports. What changes country by country is the steps, the authorities, the document checklist and when the alerts fire. Pick a country and have a look.

In plain words Same machine, different instruction card. Saudi Arabia’s steps aren’t the UAE’s steps, but a manager in either country is looking at exactly the same screens.

What a country pack actually contains

A pack is that country’s legal process written as configuration on the shared engine. The steps, the authority behind each one, the document checklist, which fields and attachments are compulsory, the payment types, and when alerts and escalations fire. They’re pre-built rather than written from scratch, which is why deployment takes weeks instead of quarters. Before go-live we run a short workshop with you to confirm document names, fees and thresholds against how your establishment actually works. Better to sort that out then than halfway through testing.

Chapter 08 · Healthcare & professional permits

A nurse needs two permissions, not one.

In healthcare the work permit is only half the story. A clinician can’t legally treat anyone until a health regulator has checked their credentials, verified them with the issuing universities and hospitals, and issued a professional licence. That runs on its own clock, with its own renewal cycle and its own training-hours requirement. We run it as a second lane on the same case.

In plain words One badge gets you into the country. A second badge lets you treat a patient. We track both, and we tell you before either one runs out.

The professional licensing lane

It runs next to the immigration lane, with its own steps, owners and expiry alerts:

  • Credentials collected at recruitment: degree, experience letters, good-standing certificate
  • Primary-source verification through DataFlow, submitted and followed to a result
  • The regulator classifies them, or assesses whether they’re eligible
  • Licensing exam booked, sat and passed, with the certificate uploaded
  • Licence issued, activated against the facility they work in, expiry recorded
  • Renewal driven by training hours, with alerts 90 days out and escalations every 15

Why it fails without a system

These are the things that go wrong when nobody is tracking it:

  • A clinician joins on a perfectly good visa but can’t be put on the roster, because their licence is still being verified
  • Verification drags on for months and nobody is watching the queue
  • A trainee pharmacist’s 45-day deadline goes past and nobody notices
  • Training hours only get logged after the renewal window has closed
  • The licence and the visa drift apart, so one lapses while the other is perfectly fine
  • The facility licence itself expires, and every practitioner working under it is exposed

Chapter 09 · Solution architecture

Every system, and what moves between them.

Here is the whole landscape on one picture. SuccessFactors stays your system of record for people. We handle the permit work. Finance gets the money. The government sits on the right, and today that connection is a person, not an API.

In plain words Four corners. Your HR system at the top, the government on the right, finance at the bottom, your people on the left. We are the bit in the middle that keeps them talking.

What flows in

Candidates who accepted an offer, the employee master, separations, and the expiry dates that start a renewal. All of it through SAP Integration Suite, so it is monitored and retried like any other SAP integration.

What flows back

Work permit and labour card numbers, Emirates ID, visa issue and expiry dates, straight into Employee Central. Nobody re-types anything, and the two systems cannot disagree.

What flows down

Every payment with its amount, VAT, reference, receipt and GL account, posted to S/4HANA as a journal. Approved fines go to payroll as deductions.

Government APIs: what we can do, and what you’ll need to get us

We built this ready for APIs. Every government-facing step is written as a service call with a slot waiting behind it, so connecting a portal is a matter of plugging it in rather than rebuilding the process. Where an authority does expose an interface, we connect it through SAP Integration Suite and the step becomes automatic: it submits, checks the status and pulls the documents back. The manual path stays there as a fallback.

Here’s what we’ll need from you. GCC governments don’t publish open APIs, and when they do give access, they give it to the employer, not to a software vendor. So you’ll need to go to each authority yourself. Qiwa and Muqeem in Saudi Arabia, MOHRE and ICP in the UAE, LMRA in Bahrain, and so on. Ask them for API credentials, the technical specification and a sandbox, under your own establishment registration. Hand those to us and we’ll build the connector. Until then the government step stays a manual job for the PRO, tracked like any other, with the reference number, receipt and status captured on the step. That’s how the system is built to work from day one, with or without a portal connection.

And honestly, that’s still the win. The value was never in automating the click on the government website. It’s in knowing the click happened, who made it, when, what it cost and what came back. That’s the bit you’re missing today.
