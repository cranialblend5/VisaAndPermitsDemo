Chapter 01 · The problem

A visa is not one job. It is forty small jobs, held by six different people.

A single work permit passes through recruitment, onboarding, the PRO desk, the employee, HR business partners and finance — crossing government portals at least five times. Miss one hand-off and the whole thing stalls silently, usually until a fine arrives.

In plain words Imagine a relay race where the runners can't see each other and nobody holds the baton list. That's how most companies run visas today. We give everyone the same list, and a whistle when someone is late.

The tracking is a spreadsheet

Expiry dates live in a file on someone's laptop. When that person is on leave, the company is blind — and overstay fines start at the border, not in the system.

The PRO has no digital desk

Government relations officers run the whole thing from WhatsApp, email and memory. There is no queue, no due date, no proof of who was asked what and when.

The money is untraceable

Typing fees, medicals, insurance and Emirates ID charges are paid on company cards and petty cash. Receipts arrive weeks later, if at all. Duplicates are found at audit.

Recruitment and PRO don't share a clock

Onboarding promises a joining date. The PRO has not been told the file exists. The candidate sits in another country waiting for an entry permit nobody started.

Compliance is a memory test

Quota availability, skill classification, profession matching, work-location alignment — all rules a human is expected to remember correctly, every time, under pressure.

Nobody can answer "where is it?"

The CHRO asks about one employee. It takes three phone calls and a day. Multiply that across thousands of permits and several operating countries.

The escalation ladder — nothing goes quiet

Step 1 · Assign

The task gets an owner

On creation the task is routed to a role and claimed by a named officer. Priority (P1–P3) and due date are stamped at that moment.

Step 2 · Nudge

Reminder every 2 days

Open tasks re-notify the assignee on a 2-day cycle. Document expiries fire 6 weeks ahead to employee, PRO and HRBP together.

Step 3 · Escalate

The manager is told

At 2 weeks before expiry — or when a task passes its due date — the line escalates to the HR Manager and line manager automatically.

Step 4 · Prove

The trail is permanent

Who was assigned, when they were reminded, what they uploaded and when they submitted is written to the record. Audits stop being archaeology.

Chapter 05 · The money trail

No payment without a receipt, a reference and a GL account.

Government fees are the leakiest part of permit management: paid on cards, in cash, through Noqodi or e-Dirham, by whoever is at the counter. The application makes the payment part of the task, so the money cannot move without leaving a record.

In plain words You can't finish the step until you've told the system what you paid, how you paid it, and uploaded the receipt. So the receipt never goes missing.
01

Captured in the step

Amount, VAT, payment mode, payment date and reference number are mandatory fields on the milestone form itself.

02

Receipt attached

The scanned receipt or invoice is a required attachment. No file, no submit — the milestone stays open.

03

Card holder recorded

Who paid and on whose card, and whether the cost is borne by the company or the employee. Reimbursement stops being a negotiation.

04

Posted to finance

Published to the correct GL account with a payment document number, ready for journal posting in S/4HANA.

05

Reportable

Filterable by process, company, employee, date range, payment mode and card holder — then exported for finance.

✓No duplicate payments

A payment reference is captured against a specific milestone of a specific case. The same fee cannot be recorded twice against the same step, and a second attempt is visible against the first.

✓No unaccounted spend

Every dirham leaves with a GL account and a payment document number attached. Finance reconciles from the report, not from a shoebox of receipts.

✓Fines have a workflow too

Overstay, traffic, labour and immigration fines are entered by the PRO, routed through the HRBP to payroll, and — once approved — stored as a payroll deduction with the HRBP copied.

Chapter 07 · Healthcare & professional permits

A nurse needs two permissions, not one.

In healthcare the work permit is only half the story. A clinician cannot legally practise until a separate health regulator has verified their credentials, run primary-source verification and issued a professional licence — on its own clock, with its own renewal cycle and its own CME requirements. The application runs that as a parallel lane on the same case.

In plain words One badge lets you into the country. A second badge lets you touch a patient. We track both — and warn you when either is about to run out.

The professional licensing lane

Tracked alongside the immigration lane, with its own milestones, owners and expiry alerts:

  • Credential pack collected at recruitment — degree, experience letters, good-standing certificate
  • Primary-source verification (DataFlow) submitted and tracked to result
  • Professional classification or eligibility assessment by the regulator
  • Licensing exam booked, sat and passed — with the certificate uploaded
  • Licence issued, activated against the employing facility, expiry recorded
  • Renewal driven by CME hours, with 90-day advance alerts and 15-day escalations

Why it fails without a system

These are the failure modes the application is designed to remove:

  • A clinician joins on a valid visa but cannot be rostered — their licence is still in verification
  • Primary-source verification takes months and nobody is watching the queue
  • A trainee pharmacist's 45-day dataflow deadline passes unnoticed
  • CME hours are not logged until the renewal window has already closed
  • Licence expiry and visa expiry drift apart, so one lapses while the other is fine
  • The facility licence itself expires — and every practitioner under it is exposed

Government API integration — what we can promise, and what you must obtain

The application is API-ready by design. Every government-facing milestone is built as an abstracted service call, so a portal integration drops into an existing slot rather than requiring the workflow to be rebuilt. Where an authority exposes an interface, we connect it through SAP Integration Suite and the step becomes automatic — submission, status polling and document retrieval — with the manual path kept as fallback.

What we need from you. GCC government portals do not publish open APIs. Access is granted to the employer, not to the software vendor — so the customer must approach each authority directly (Qiwa and Muqeem in KSA, MOHRE and ICP in the UAE, LMRA in Bahrain, and so on) and obtain API credentials, a technical specification and a sandbox under their own establishment registration. Once you hand us those credentials and the spec, we build the connector. Until then — and this is how the system runs successfully in production today — the government step stays a tracked manual PRO activity, with the reference number, receipt and status captured in the milestone.

Why this still wins. The value is not in automating the click on the government site. It is in knowing that the click happened, who made it, when, what it cost and what came back — which is precisely what is missing today.
